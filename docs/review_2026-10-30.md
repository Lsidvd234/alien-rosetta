# Review of "Alien Rosetta" (state of 30 Oct 2026)

Reviewer stance: strict course instructor + ML-venue reviewer. Numbers marked **[checked]** were recomputed by me from
result files (CPU only); everything else is taken from the project files as written.

## 1. Research question and contribution

The project asks what a small pretrained LM (Qwen3-0.6B-Base + LoRA) needs in order to learn translation into an
invented, Tamil-like language written in new glyph tokens, and where that learning stops. It does this with a
generator that gives exact answer keys and an intermediate "meaning" line. The contribution is a controlled testbed
plus a set of mostly confirmatory findings:
- symbol embeddings initialised from what they denote help a lot;
- generalisation tracks training coverage of structures, facts and words, with dose curves for passives and for
  multiplication facts;
- real English text beats synthetic English;
- on out-of-distribution English most failures sit in the meaning line, not the glyphs;
- output features can be steered along linear directions.

The framing is clear but broad, and it drifted. The charter (PLAN.md §2A) started as a tokenization/predict-then-test
study. It is now 7 claims plus a candidate 8th, and they do not add up to one thesis.

## 2. Claim by claim

**Claim 1 (start symbols from their meaning).**
- (a) Language, v1, one seed: sound start 0.990/0.986/0.972/0.974 vs random-word copy 0.570/0.506/0.420/0.000
  [checked against `ALL_RUNS.md`/`final_20k.md`]. The proper control (wrong sound) reaches 0.982/0.978/0.860/0.650.
  So a distinct letter-like start gives the in-distribution gain, and the correct sound adds only generalisation
  (morph +0.11, long +0.32, McNemar p=3e-42 on long).
  Maths: 0.86 vs 0.34 vs 0.09 (two-seed means, add 4+4).
- (b) The wording is mostly fine. But `PROJECT_OVERVIEW.md` §4 calls the 0.57 run the "control run with wrong sounds".
  That is **wrong**: the wrong-sound control is the 0.98 run.
- (c) The project's own swap test shows a wrong start's outcome depends hugely on the specific permutation
  (0.05–0.60). The language wrong-sound control is a **single derangement, single seed**, so its 0.65 on "long" is one
  draw from a high-variance distribution. "s1" is a deterministic re-run, not a replicate.
- (d) **Moderate** (language), **moderate** (maths). Project: strong / moderate-strong. I disagree on the language
  side.

**Claim 2 (coverage of structure).**
- (a) Held-out structures score 0.00–0.09. Passive dose 0/50/300 gives 0.00/0.53/0.75. Dropped multiplication fact:
  0.05 vs 0.84 without swapped twins (twin split checked in findings).
- (b) "Generalisation stops at what training covers" overreaches. The model composes new word forms (0.84–0.86),
  answers 6×5 from 5×6 (0.99), and passives jump to 0.53 with 50 examples (0.06% of data). The data support
  "structures with zero coverage fail; a small dose suffices".
- (c) In maths the "never seen" fact is known from pretraining in decimal (5·6=30), and conversion examples
  (30 → "42") are in training. What is missing is the base-7 multiplication record, not the fact.
- (d) **Strong** descriptively; the causal dose is **moderate** (single seed, 3 points). Project: strong; I partly
  agree.

**Claim 3 (vocabulary).**
- (a) Floor 25 gives +0.11 on generated text (p=2e-5), but with 22% more examples/steps. Size-matched control pending.
  Null on Tatoeba and sealed (sealed McNemar 6/2, 9/3; p 0.29/0.15 [checked]). Loanwords: one-letter rules transfer
  partially (0.23 vs 0.12, p=0.005).
- (c) The data-size confound is unresolved, and 6 epochs alone gave +0.05.
- (d) **Weak** until n98k returns. Project: moderate.

**Claim 4 (real text).**
- (a) Tatoeba +0.307/+0.303 (189/5 and 186/4 discordant; p 1.8e-49 / 6.8e-50 [checked]).
  Sealed +0.082/+0.095 (20/8 and 21/7; p 0.036/0.013; seeds together 22/10, p 0.050 [checked]).
  Hand-check: 30/49 vs 13/20, Fisher p=1.0; 116 of 189 gains rest on a correct gold (95% range 89–139), i.e.
  +0.19 [0.15, 0.23] [checked].
- (b) "Closes much of the gap" holds on Tatoeba, which is in-distribution for the training sentences (same source;
  I found almost no near-duplicate analyses, so this is distribution match, not leakage). On sealed, +0.09 is modest.
- (c) **The sealed gold is not independent of the reader.** The sealed meanings were annotated "reader plus hand
  corrections" (report §5.3), with the same reader that labelled real15's 12k training sentences. Real15 can gain on
  sealed by learning reader conventions that survived the 52 corrections. The everyday run (+0.04/+0.05 on sealed,
  reader-free) is the cleaner lower bound.
- (d) **Strong** on Tatoeba agreement, **moderate-weak** on true real-English quality. Project: strong / moderate;
  roughly agree.

**Claim 5 (failures move from writing to reading; post hoc).**
- (a) The reading share of failures rises from ~0.5 (generated) to 0.70 (Tatoeba) and 0.88 (sealed) for the baseline.
  I recomputed it from `error_split.md`: Tatoeba (14+10+14)/(38+16)=0.70; sealed 50/57=0.88.
- (b) "Same order in all 10 models" overreaches: no model is scored on all five families. The v2 models cover three,
  the v1 models the two ends.
- (c) **The ratio is mechanically confounded.** A writing failure can only be counted when the analysis is exactly
  right, so as reading accuracy falls the share of reading failures must rise even if writing is unchanged. The
  meaningful quantities are P(analysis wrong) and P(glyphs wrong | analysis right). The latter is 0.24 (generated),
  0.26 (Tatoeba), 0.15 (sealed) for the baseline: flat or falling. So the real finding is "reading degrades under
  shift; conditional writing does not", which is simpler and less novel.
  Also, "word not in language" (14% of Tatoeba items) is filed as a reading failure, though it is arguably
  target-vocabulary knowledge. Moving it to writing drops the Tatoeba reading share to 0.44, below generated.
- (d) **Weak-moderate** as worded. Project: strong. **Disagree.**

**Claim 6 (tricks are not the fix).**
- (a) Focal −0.028 (p=0.014), 6 epochs −0.053 (p=1.7e-4), beam/vote null [all checked].
- (b) The tricks are weak and one-shot each (one γ, a 0.6B rewriter, 1.4%/8% paraphrase). The obvious "trick", model
  size, is still pending.
- (d) **Weak-moderate**: "these tricks did not help". Project: moderate.

**Claim 7 (English meaning directions drive the output; post hoc).**
- (a) Mean-difference directions at layer 6 flip tense/negation at ≥0.91 vs ≤0.02 random, on 4 models.
  Subject pushes "repair" 11/12 and 10/10 errors.
- (b) "Not from a separate one" is untested; no alternative architecture was specified.
- (c) Several problems:
  - Any translator must read tense from the input, so causal dependence on a linear tense feature of the input states
    is nearly guaranteed.
  - The setting (108 combinations) was picked on the same PRES→PAST test sentences later reported as 1.00
    (`inside_claim4_steer2.py` l.203–219).
  - The random-direction control is weak in d=1024.
  - The "repairs" push toward the gold value (an oracle), so they show controllability, not latent knowledge.
  - Directions are called "probe directions" but are class-mean differences.
- (d) **Moderate**. Project: strong (causal). **Disagree.**

**Candidate claim 8 (role rule, maths).**
- (a) One seed: mixed building rules 0.77 needs-pair; steps via blocks seen as blocks 0.996 vs 0.17 otherwise.
- (c) The route split is not randomised: the model chooses its own route, so the 0.996/0.17 split is confounded with
  its confidence.
- (d) **Weak** (single seed, replicates pending). The gate RC1–RC3 is well designed.

## 3. Methodology and statistics

- **Leakage.**
  - Generated tests exclude training sentences by exact English string only (`represent_v2.py:1560`), so "iid"
    items can share a meaning with training items. This is minor, since iid isn't the claim.
  - Tatoeba test/train is a random sentence split (`scripts/tatoeba_split.py`). Exact analysis overlap is 1 of 600,
    and only 8% have a closest training analysis with Jaccard ≥0.6 **[checked]**: acceptable.
  - Maths: `needs_pair` correctly removes both orders, and the scratchpad leak checks are explicit (`maths.py:645–662`).
  - Default data is byte-identical with switches off (asserted in comments and findings). I did not rerun this.
- **Gold labels.** With ~60% correct Tatoeba gold, the absolute Tatoeba numbers (baseline 0.62, real15 0.92) are not
  interpretable as accuracy, and the 20 control sentences are themselves only 65% right. All Tatoeba-based mechanism
  analyses (claim 3 rare words, claim 5 Tatoeba, probes "where output wrong") inherit this.
  The exact-string analysis metric (`run.py:325`) also turns convention mismatches into "misreadings".
- **Sealed set.** 147 items; a single comparison resolves only about ±0.11. Paired McNemar is the right test.
  However, the switch to a paired primary test came after the unpaired and pooled tests had been seen (and the pooled
  one withdrawn). Written before computing, yes, but chosen after a null: a forking path. The author wrote the
  sentences, designed the generator, annotated the gold and later chose the everyday word list. "Totals only" does
  not remove the author's memory of the sentences.
- **Seeds.** The passive dose, floor 25, everyday, claim-8 and stage-1 runs are all single-seed. Only claim 4 and the
  steering have true training-seed replicates.
- **Multiple comparisons.** There are dozens of tests and no family-wise correction. The Tatoeba results survive any
  correction; sealed p=0.036, everyday-vs-s1 p=0.039 and the sign test p=0.050 would not survive Holm.
- **Pre-registration.** The notebook order is consistent: predictions precede results, amendments are dated (P1
  amended before running; R4 amended after the pilot and labelled exploratory), and failures are reported (R2b, R3,
  S2/S3, RB1, RB3, C5v2, the swap control). This is unusually honest.
  But the project is **not under version control**, so the order cannot be verified externally. Some thresholds are
  reinterpreted after the fact ("set badly", "fails in an informative way"), and claim 8 was "dropped" and then
  revived by new designs.
  The post-hoc labels on claims 5 and 7 are present and clear.
- **Probes.** R4's binding result (probe 0.746 vs best baseline 0.704) is called a "representation failure". But the
  untrained base model, which reads passives, also scores 0.704: the probe cannot detect the doer even where it must
  be represented. Without a positive control (e.g. the baseline that reads passives) R4 is **uninformative**, not
  negative. A non-significant 0.04 gap on ~240 items is also treated as evidence of absence.

## 5. Biggest weaknesses (ranked) and fixes

1. **Measurement validity of every real-English claim.** Tatoeba gold is ~60% right, sealed gold is reader-derived,
   one annotator, exact-string metric.
   *Fix:* get a second annotator for the sealed gold and 150 Tatoeba items and report agreement (κ). Report
   field-level F1 alongside exact match. Re-state claim 4 on reader-free comparisons (everyday vs baseline).
   ~2 days, no GPU.
2. **Claim 5 rests on a ratio that rises by construction.**
   *Fix:* report P(analysis wrong) and P(glyphs wrong | analysis right) per family, with "word not in language"
   separate. Let the pending reader/writer pipeline (BN1–BN3) carry the claim. Reword it. Half a day of CPU.
3. **Interpretability claims overreach.**
   *Fix:* add a positive control for R4 (baseline/passive300 model), held-out sentences for the steering-setting
   search, control directions of other features, and the word "oracle" for the repairs. Rate claim 7 moderate.
   ~1 day on the laptop GPU.
4. **Single seeds on the headline causal results.**
   *Fix:* a second seed of passive0/passive50 (~12 GPU-h); 2–3 wrong-sound derangements for the v1 claim-1 control
   (~2 GPU-h each at 50k); the claim-8 replicates (queued). Apply Holm across the claim-level tests. Fits the
   31 Oct quota if the sound-rule and trick/split/double runs are dropped.
5. **Too many claims, and scope creep.** 8 claims, ~20 maths variants queued 2 weeks before the deadline.
   *Fix:* freeze runs now and cut to 3–4 claims (symbol init, coverage + dose, real text, reading bottleneck). Move
   grokking, loanwords and the tricks to an appendix. Rename claim 6 "these tricks". 1–2 days of writing.

Also check: the 31 Oct plan uses three Kaggle accounts. Kaggle's terms allow one account per person; make sure the
second and third accounts are other people's, used with their consent.

## 6. What is genuinely strong

1. **The testbed design.** An exact answer key, a meaning line that separates reading from writing, and many
   independent switches (dose, hold-out, init) that keep default data byte-identical. That is the real asset.
2. **Honesty and self-correction.** Withdrawn results, failed predictions and code-review bugs are all logged. Few
   student projects, and not all papers, do this.
3. **Clean dose experiments.** The passive 0/50/300 and dropped-fact-with-twin-split designs are proper interventions
   with built-in controls.
4. **Correct statistics where it matters.** Paired McNemar on the same items, bootstrap CIs, and a hand-check that
   quantifies label noise instead of ignoring it. All my recomputations matched.
5. **Diagnosing the shuffled-start variance** with a permutation swap test: a small, crisp causal experiment.
